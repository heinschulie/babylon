# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Babylon serves two connected groups. Learners are people building spoken isiXhosa confidence through short, repeatable pronunciation practice, often on phones and in interrupted daily contexts. Human verifiers are reviewers who listen to learner attempts, score pronunciation across clear dimensions, and provide exemplar audio when AI feedback needs calibration or human trust.

## Product Purpose

Babylon helps learners practice isiXhosa pronunciation by recording phrases, receiving AI feedback, revisiting material through spaced repetition, and, when available, getting human verification. Success means learners can hear what to improve, repeat the right material at the right time, and trust the feedback enough to keep practicing. For verifiers, success means fast, fair review work with clear scoring criteria and low ambiguity.

## Brand Personality

Energetic, warm, bold. The interface should feel alive and encouraging without becoming childish. It should carry the confidence of a serious learning tool while making spoken practice feel approachable.

## Anti-references

Avoid corporate edtech, generic SaaS learning dashboards, and Duolingo-style gamified cuteness. Do not lean on mascot-driven motivation, patronizing streak mechanics, stock classroom imagery, or bland enterprise training patterns.

## Design Principles

1. Make spoken practice feel active: recording, feedback, and review states should have a clear sense of progress and momentum.
2. Earn trust through clarity: scores, AI feedback, human review status, billing limits, and next actions must be explicit.
3. Warm without cute: use encouragement, tactile UI, and bold hierarchy without infantilizing learners or the language.
4. Respect linguistic depth: theory, pronunciation detail, and isiXhosa content should feel precise, not simplified into novelty.
5. Keep review work focused: verifier screens should reduce ambiguity, foreground deadlines and scoring criteria, and avoid decorative distraction.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA. Maintain strong contrast in all themes, support keyboard access across recording and review flows, respect reduced-motion preferences, and avoid relying on color alone for score or status meaning. Interfaces should work well on mobile, especially for learner recording sessions, and should treat isiXhosa text as first-class content rather than a secondary translation.
